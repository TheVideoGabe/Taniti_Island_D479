TANITI

A small, tropical island in the Pacific.

INFORMATION

Taniti has an indigenous population of about 20,000. Until a recent increase in tourism, most of the Tanitian economy was dominated by fishing or agriculture.

While the island has an area of less than 500 square miles, the terrain is varied and includes both sandy and rocky beaches, a small but safe harbor, lush tropical rain forests, and a mountainous interior that includes a small, active volcano.

ACTIVITIES

Visit our local history museums, go on chartered fishing tours, snorkeling, zip-lining in the rain forest.

Try visiting our local pubs, including a microbrewery, dance at our new dance club, take helicopter rides and check out our art galleries.

Most people visit Taniti to enjoy the beaches, explore the rain forest, and to visit the small active volcano.

FOOD

Taniti currently has 10 restaurants: five serve mostly local fish and rice, three serve American-style meals, and two serve Pan-Asian cuisine.

Taniti has two supermarkets, two smaller grocery stores, and one convenience store that is open 24 hours a day.

TRANSPORTATION

Come visit us by airplane or maybe stop on the way in a cruise ship. There are many ways here and take advantage of what we have to offer.

ACCOMMODATIONS

If you need a place to rest or spend the rest of your stay, we have you covered.

Taniti has a wide variety of lodging that ranges from inexpensive hostels to one large, four-star resort. There are many small, family-owned hotels and a growing number of bed and breakfasts. All types of lodging are strictly regulated and regularly inspected by the Tanitian government.

FAQ

Power outlets are 120 volts, the same as in the United States.

Taniti uses the U.S. dollar as its currency, but many businesses also accept euros and yen.

Many younger Tanitians speak fluent English. Very little English is spoken in rural areas, especially by older residents.

The drinking age on Taniti is 18 and the drinking age is not strictly enforced.
